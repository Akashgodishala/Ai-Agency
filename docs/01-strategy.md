# 01 — Strategy

> The goal of this document: get crystal clear on **what business you're in**,
> **who you sell to**, and **how you make money** — before spending a dollar or
> writing any code.

---

## 1. The two businesses (and why we pick one first)

"An AI agency that builds agents for any use case" is two businesses:

**A) Services agency (done-for-you).** Clients come to you. You build and deliver
custom agents for them, like a consultancy or a marketing agency. Humans + AI do
the work behind the scenes.

**B) Software platform (self-serve).** You build software where a client types
their use case and the system generates the agent automatically. This is a
product/SaaS company.

**We start with A. We grow B out of A.** Here's why:

- A can start **this week** with almost no money. B needs months of engineering
  and a team you don't have yet.
- A gives you **paying customers and cash flow immediately**. B gives you
  nothing until it's built.
- Running A teaches you **which use cases actually repeat**. You can't build the
  right platform (B) until you've delivered the same thing by hand 20 times.
- Every strong AI product company you admire did services/manual delivery first,
  then productized. This is the proven path, not a compromise.

> **Mental model:** Do it by hand → notice the pattern → build the template →
> the template library becomes the product. Services fund the product.

---

## 2. Pick a niche (do not skip this)

The instinct is "we serve everyone with any use case." That instinct will make
you invisible. A generalist "we do any AI for anyone" agency has no referrals, no
reusable templates, and competes with everyone.

**Pick ONE niche to start.** You can expand later. A good starter niche is:

- A group of businesses that **have money and a clear painful, repetitive task**.
- A group you can **reach** (you know people in it, or they're easy to find).
- A group where **one solution resells** to the next customer (same use case
  repeats → you get faster and cheaper each time).

### Candidate niches (all proven demand)

| Niche | Painful repetitive task an agent can solve |
|-------|--------------------------------------------|
| Real estate agencies | Lead follow-up, answering listing questions, booking viewings |
| E-commerce / DTC brands | Customer support, order status, product Q&A, review responses |
| Law firms / solo attorneys | Intake screening, document summarizing, client FAQ |
| Medical & dental clinics | Appointment booking, reminders, patient FAQ, insurance questions |
| Recruiting / staffing | Resume screening, candidate outreach, scheduling |
| Local services (HVAC, dental, salons) | Missed-call follow-up, booking, quote requests |
| Coaches / course creators | Lead qualification, content repurposing, community support |
| Accounting / bookkeeping firms | Client document intake, categorization, reminders |

> **You don't have to know the answer yet.** I'll help you choose based on who
> you can actually reach today. Whoever you already have contacts or industry
> knowledge in is usually the fastest path to a first client.

## 3. The offer (what you actually sell)

Sell **outcomes**, not "AI agents." Nobody wakes up wanting an agent; they want
their problem gone. Frame every offer as a business result:

- ❌ "We build you a custom AI agent."
- ✅ "We make sure every lead gets a reply within 60 seconds, 24/7, so you stop
  losing deals to slow follow-up — no new hire needed."

### Productized offer structure

Don't do fully bespoke pricing at first — it's slow and scary to quote. Offer a
small **menu of packaged agents** (see [Agent Catalog](03-agent-catalog.md)),
each with a fixed setup fee and a monthly fee.

## 4. Pricing model

The agency-standard model that works: **Setup fee + monthly retainer.**

| Tier | Setup (one-time) | Monthly | What's included |
|------|------------------|---------|-----------------|
| Starter | $500–$1,500 | $200–$500/mo | One agent, one use case, basic support |
| Growth | $2,000–$5,000 | $500–$1,500/mo | 2–3 agents, integrations, priority support |
| Custom | $5,000+ | $1,500+/mo | Multiple agents, custom workflows, SLAs |

Why this model:
- **Setup fee** covers your build time and filters out non-serious buyers.
- **Monthly retainer** = predictable recurring revenue. It pays for the AI usage
  costs, monitoring, tweaks, and support — and it's what makes the business
  valuable over time.

> **Rule of thumb:** your monthly price should be a small fraction of what the
> agent saves or earns the client. If your support agent saves them a $3,000/mo
> hire, $500/mo is an easy yes.

Start at the **lower end** for your first 1–3 clients (call them "founding
clients") in exchange for a testimonial and case study. Then raise prices.

## 5. Positioning & competitive edge

You're not competing on "we have AI" — everyone claims that. Your edge is:

1. **Niche expertise** — "we build AI support agents *specifically for dental
   clinics*" beats "we build AI." You speak their language.
2. **Speed & done-for-you** — clients don't want to learn tools; they want the
   result handed to them, running.
3. **Reliability & support** — most AI demos break in the real world. Your moat
   is that yours *keeps working* and someone answers when it doesn't.

---

## 6. What "world's top" realistically looks like (staged)

Ambition is good; here's the honest ladder to it:

- **Stage 1 — Freelancer/agency (Month 1–3):** You + AI tools deliver agents by
  hand to your first niche. Goal: 3–5 paying clients, testimonials.
- **Stage 2 — Real agency (Month 4–9):** Repeatable templates, 1–2
  contractors/hires, 10–20 clients, case studies, referrals.
- **Stage 3 — Productized / platform (Month 9–18+):** The repeated templates get
  a self-serve front end. Clients start onboarding themselves. This is where "top
  platform" begins — funded by the revenue from Stages 1–2.

You reach the top by climbing this ladder, not by skipping to the top rung.
